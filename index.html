Camera setup

Welcome to Orbital. Enter your camera's address and login (the same as for its web interface) and press Save and connect. Everything else can usually stay as it is.

Streams, ports and network

Audio

The camera has no microphone, only a line input (switch it on in the camera web interface). Alternatively, use an audio input of this computer – e.g. a USB audio interface: the server adds it to the video through go2rtc and ffmpeg, so it is part of the live view and of every recording.

Changing the source restarts the video service for a moment; it is not possible while recording. Audio from a device on this computer needs ffmpeg (same as stabilization). It can be slightly ahead of the camera image, which has a few hundred milliseconds of delay.

Accelerate/brake: time to full speed and to standstill. S-shape 0 % is a straight ramp, 100 % a fully smooth curve. Set the camera's own acceleration in its web interface as short as possible, otherwise both curves add up.

Presets & moves

Straight line: the server drives stored presets itself over VISCA, with a smooth S-curve and both axes together – the way a camera operator would pan. On its own the camera finishes tilt too early (bent path). Presets learn their VISCA coordinates when they are saved or first recalled.

Other presets stored in the camera

Presets are stored in the camera and also work with its remote and other software. Names and VISCA targets live in presets.json. To name or save a preset, use the pencil next to the presets; there you can also give each preset its own move time (otherwise the settings above apply).

Stabilizing measures the camera motion frame by frame, computes a calm path from it and shifts the slightly cropped image along. The jolts at the camera's speed levels and hard starts become smooth transitions; intended pans are kept. The result is saved as …_smooth.mp4 next to the original, which stays untouched. Processing takes roughly one to two times the length of the recording.

Calibration

Measure motion: for about 70 seconds the camera makes short pans, tilts and zooms on its own – over both control paths and at different speeds – while the server records. The recording and a log end up in the recording folder. Point the camera at something with texture first (not a blank wall).

VISCA check: queries the position over all VISCA paths and moves the camera in small steps for about 20 seconds. Result: visca_check.json.
